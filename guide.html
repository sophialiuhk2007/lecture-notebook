How to make the most of Lecture Notebook

A notebook for math and statistics lectures: type formulas quickly, keep definitions and theorems tidy, write by hand, read the course PDFs beside your notes, and study from it all later. It runs in your browser with no account.

Getting started

  1. Open the notebook. The first time, a welcome card offers a one-minute tour. You can replay it any time from Export → ✦ Take the tour or the ? help panel.
  2. Make a course and a note. In the left sidebar, Course creates a class (e.g. “Stats 101”), + Lecture starts typed notes titled with today’s date, and Handwritten starts pages for a stylus.
  3. Type. Click anywhere in the note and write. Everything saves in your browser as you go; the pill in the top bar says when it’s saved.
  4. Optional extras. ⚙ Settings (top right) connects an AI for the ✦ tools and Google Drive to back up and use your notes on other devices. You can skip both.
On iPad

Share → Add to Home Screen opens it full screen like an app. Apple Pencil works in Safari.

Typing math

Formulas are typed in Typst, a math syntax that is shorter than LaTeX. Type mk and a space (or Tab) to start a formula, dm for a displayed one, or put it between $ … $. The finished math floats above while you type; press Esc, Enter or arrow out to turn it back into math. Click a formula to edit it again.

Quick shortcuts inside a formula

xsr → x², cb → ³, invs → ⁻¹, sq → √( ), x1 → x₁. Backspace right after a shortcut undoes it. Letters next to each other are one name, so write a b for a·b (xbar and thetahat still work).

Math in ordinary sentences

Outside a formula your words stay words, but a few phrases become symbols: expected value of X → E[X], variance of X → Var(X), sum from i=1 to n → Σ, limit as n goes to infinity → lim, X ~ N → ∼, -> <= >= != → → ≤ ≥ ≠, Greek names → letters. x_i^2 makes real sub- and superscripts. Select words and type $ to turn them into math.

Know LaTeX already? $\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$ works too. The ∑ button (⌘;) lists every symbol and stats shortcut.

Blocks: definitions, theorems, equations…

A note is a list of blocks. Plain paragraphs (with lists and inline math) form one text block; headings, boxes, numbered equations, pictures, tables and drawings are their own blocks. Start a line with a prefix to make one:

def: A random vector X is Multivariate Normal if t′X is Normal for every t.
thm: The distribution of X is determined by the distributions of all t′X.
pf: “of Theorem 3” in the title links the proof to its theorem.

Moving, selecting and copying blocks

  • Each block has a ⠿ handle on its left when you point at it. Drag it to move the block; click it to select the block.
  • Shift-click selects a range, ⌘-click adds one block, ⌘A selects all.
  • With blocks selected: ⌫ delete, ⌘C copy, ⌘X cut, ⌘V paste, ⌘D duplicate, ⌥↑/⌥↓ move.
  • Copied blocks paste back as the same blocks, with their types, even in another note, another tab or after a reload.
  • Right-click a block (or its ⋯) for its menu: change type, turn into a comment, mark it, ask the AI about it.

References and marks

Write “by Theorem 3”, “Def 2”, “eq (1)” or “§2”. When you leave the block it becomes a live link that follows renumbering. Or type @ and pick. Hover a link to see an arrow to what it points at.

⚑ Mark (⌘⇧K) tags a block as to-do, double-check, ask teacher, try it, important or possible error. The Marks tab in the sidebar collects them across your notes, so nothing gets forgotten before an exam.

Comments

  • Select text and press 💬 (⌘⇧M) to comment in the margin. Reply, resolve, or ask the AI from a comment.
  • Right-click a block → 💬 Turn into a comment moves a side thought into the margin. It keeps the colour of its block type; ↩ Block puts it back.
  • The speech-bubble button in the top bar hides or shows all comments. PDFs have their own comment sidebar.

Handwriting

  • Handwritten in the sidebar makes a note of paper pages (lined, grid or plain) for a stylus or mouse: pen, highlighter, shapes, lasso, text and palm rejection. A new page appears as you reach the bottom.
  • Point at a page number for ＋ (add a page after) and the bin (delete it; undo brings it back).
  • ⛶ Full screen gives the pages the whole window; comments work there too.
  • ✎ in the top bar opens scratch paper beside typed notes. Insert it into the note when it’s worth keeping.
  • ✦ Convert to typed notes (needs AI) makes a linked typed copy with math and boxes; your handwriting stays untouched.
  • ⤓ Save as PDF exports the pages.

Readings (PDFs)

📖 keeps each course’s PDFs (slides, book chapters, problem sets) next to your notes. Readings belong to a course, so give the note a course first.

  • Select text in a PDF to quote it into your notes or highlight it.
  • ✎ Pen writes on the page; ▭ Box draws a box around a formula or figure to comment on it or ask the AI about it.
  • ⛶ Full screen fills the window with the reading and puts the chat beside it. Esc goes back.
  • Your marks stay with the reading and show in every note of that course.

AI help (optional)

The ✦ tools explain a passage, give intuition, build interactive demos, check a proof, clean up rough notes (your raw copy stays in Version history), read handwriting and whiteboard photos, and turn PDFs into notes. Ask opens a chat that sees the current note, the block you’re on, your open reading page and the course’s readings.

Study and review

  • Study hides definitions and theorems: click one or press Space to reveal it.
  • Review turns definitions, theorems and starred blocks into flashcards.
  • The All / Main / Results switch shows everything, just the lecture (asides hidden), or a cheat sheet of headings, definitions, theorems and equations.
  • ◌ Aside mode marks what you add while reviewing (extra steps, whiteboard photos) so it stays separate from the lecture, labelled with the date. Merge moves an aside into the notes.
  • Side by side reads another note in a right-hand panel; the Outline tab jumps between headings.

Export

  • ⤓ Download PDF: sharp text and math, your choice of page size. 📄 PDF printout gives pages you can draw on.
  • LaTeX (.zip): notes.tex plus images, ready for Overleaf. LaTeX for handing in uses plain amsthm style with no colours, asides or comments, ideal for problem sets.
  • ⤒ LaTeX file / snippet goes the other way: a .tex file (e.g. a problem set) becomes blocks.

Backups and using several devices

Notes live in this browser on this device. To keep them safe and use them elsewhere:

Google Drive (recommended)

⚙ Settings → Google Drive → Connect Google Drive and sign in. Every note backs up to a Lecture Notebook backups folder in your Drive a few minutes after you stop typing. On another device, connect the same Google account and use ☁ → Load from Drive…. Loaded notes stay linked: edits on either device reach the other. Load as a copy makes an unlinked copy instead. The app can see only the files it creates, not the rest of your Drive.

Google sign-in lasts about an hour. If backups pause, click the ☁ Drive button → Back up now.

A backup file

Export → ⤓ Back up whole notebook saves a .zip with every note, drawing, picture and PDF (or, if you choose, everything but the PDFs). A big notebook comes out as several parts of about 250 MB. Export → ⤒ Restore from a backup file adds them to any browser: pick all the parts at once. This also moves notes from the claude.ai version of the notebook to the website.

Clearing this site’s data in your browser deletes the notes stored here. Keep Drive backup on, or save a backup file now and then.

Privacy

  • There’s no account and no server of ours storing your notes. They stay in your browser.
  • Google Drive backup goes to your Drive, with access only to the files this app makes.
  • AI requests go from your browser straight to the provider you chose, with your key. Harvard requests pass through a small relay that adds the web headers Harvard’s gateway lacks; it stores nothing.
  • Settings and keys are kept only in this browser.

FAQ

Do I need an AI key?

No. Writing, math, handwriting, readings, comments, study mode, flashcards, export and backups all work without one. Only the ✦ tools and Ask need it.

Where did my notes go?

Notes are per browser. A different browser, a private window or another device starts empty. Use Google Drive (Load from Drive) or a backup file to bring them over.

Google says “Access blocked” or the sign-in window closes

Allow pop-ups for the site and use a regular browser window (not an in-app browser). Some school or work Google accounts block apps their admin hasn’t approved; a personal Gmail account works.

My Harvard key isn’t accepted

Paste the app’s Key, not the Secret, and check the HUIT portal shows the app’s OpenAI access as approved.

Can I use it offline?

Opening the site needs a connection. Once it’s open, you can keep writing if the Wi-Fi drops (notes save in the browser); AI needs a connection, and Drive backups catch up when you’re back online.

Can I share a note?

Export it as a PDF or LaTeX, or back it up to Drive and share that file from Drive.
